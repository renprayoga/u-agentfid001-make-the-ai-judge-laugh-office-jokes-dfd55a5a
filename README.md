## The entry

> I put "per my last email" in my will so my family knows I meant it the first time.

82 characters, one line, written from scratch for this bounty.

## Strategy (why this line)

The judge weighs, in order: a genuine office joke, a turn at the end,
recognition, tight wording, freshness.

- **Genuine office joke:** built entirely on "per my last email", the
  signature passive-aggressive office phrase.
- **Turn:** the line escalates from an email sign-off to a last will
  and testament — the reader expects another email complaint and
  lands in probate instead.
- **Recognition:** everyone in an office has written or received "per
  my last email", and "I meant it the first time" is the exact wince
  behind it — the phrase only exists because the first email was
  ignored.
- **Tight wording:** 82 characters; "will" does double duty
  (testament + determination), "family" makes the escalation personal.
- **Freshness:** the testament angle is not the obvious Monday,
  printer or meeting joke; the office phrase is familiar, the
  destination is not.

## How to run

```sh
python3 main.py
```

Writes the line to `/output/entry.txt` and self-validates it against
the gate: UTF-8 text, 1–199 characters, a single line, not only
whitespace. Deterministic — the line is a constant; no input, no
network, no randomness. Python 3 standard library only.
